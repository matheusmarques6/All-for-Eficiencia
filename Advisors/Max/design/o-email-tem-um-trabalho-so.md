# A tese

> Pretty doesn't equal profit.
> Your email has one job: **Get the click.**
> Here's how to design your emails to hand the click to the customer on a
> silver platter: (L8131-8133, verbatim)

Na fala ele qualifica um pouco: "Pretty does not always equal profit, okay? Long
term, we do want pretty emails, but our email at the end of the day has one job.
Get the click" (L7011-7013). O deck corta o "always" e o "long term".

# As sete regras (slide, verbatim)

> * **Button Above the Fold** – Most users won't scroll. Put a CTA button in the hero section so every reader sees it immediately.
> * **Large, Thumb-Friendly Buttons** – Think mobile-first. Buttons should be at least 1.5–2 inches wide so they're easy to tap.
> * **Clear, Repeating CTAs** – Don't just place one button at the end. Repeat your CTA 2–3 times throughout the email.
> * **Center Your Buttons** – Most people hold their phone in their right hand. Centered buttons reduce friction and boost taps.
> * **Buttons for Each Product** – If you're showing multiple products, each one should have its own "Shop Now" or "View Product" button underneath.
> * **End with a General CTA** – Not every product will land. Always close with a "Shop All" or "Browse More" button for catch-all clicks.
> * **Make CTAs Stand Out** – Use high-contrast colors and simple backgrounds. Your buttons should be the most obvious thing in the email. (L8135-8141)

# O racional falado, regra por regra

1. **Above the fold** — a maioria abre no celular, olha a seção de cima e sai;
   se é só isso que vê, precisa ter onde clicar (L7017-7023). Mas logo em
   seguida ele afrouxa: "Button above the fold for 75% of your email. Don't need
   to include it in every single one, but I'd recommend majority of them"
   (L7027-7029). Ver [[secao-hero]] e o conflito abaixo.
2. **Tamanho** — mede pelo polegar, não pelo layout: "Look at how big this
   button is. Even a blind person could click that" (L7039). E o argumento de
   teste: "I've never had a bigger button lose an A-B test in click rates. The
   bigger the button, the better" (L7041-7043).
3. **Repetir CTA** — para que quem já rolou não precise voltar ao topo
   (L7055-7057). O contrapeso é dele: "Don't confuse that with throwing a shit
   ton of buttons in your email. That's going to overwhelm the customer"
   (L7059-7061). A fala não dá número; o slide dá 2-3.
4. **Centralizar** — e as exceções são só da fala: imagem com a pessoa do lado
   direito, e seções de produto em zigue-zague (L7071-7081). "But for main
   buttons, try to keep them as centered as possible because oftentimes that
   will get more clicks" (L7081-7083).
5. **Botão por produto** — a estatística de auditoria dele: "Like 75% of the
   brands I audit don't have individual shop now buttons for specific products"
   (L7089). Ver [[secao-product]].
6. **CTA geral no fim** — pega quem rolou tudo e não gostou de nenhum produto
   (L7105-7115). Ver [[secao-product]] e [[secao-footer]].
7. **Saltar aos olhos** — alto contraste, fundo simples; na fala ele acrescenta
   uma técnica: "We like to add backdrops a lot on our buttons right here. So it
   kind of like has a 3D effect and makes it stand out" (L7047-7049).
