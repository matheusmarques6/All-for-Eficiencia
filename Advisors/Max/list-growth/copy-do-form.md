# O que é

As frases que ele testou e usa nos pop-ups. **Verbatim, em inglês. Não
traduzir** — o efeito de cada uma está na palavra escolhida, não no sentido.
Todas vêm da aula 6 (L986-1083), que é a transcrição de um vídeo público, não da
aula do curso.

# CTA do botão de submit

> ~~Continue~~ → **Claim now**

Racional: "if somebody sees limited time 10% off and they see a button that says
continue, continue implies that there's going to be more that the person has to
do. So instead, we want it to make it seem like, hey, you will claim your
discount right now if you click this button" (L1037-1038). No passo de SMS o
equivalente é **Claim discount** (L1039).

# Headline da oferta

> ~~Limited time 10% off~~ → **You've got 10% off**

Racional, dois efeitos empilhados (L1042-1043):

1. Personalização — "limited time 10% off just doesn't feel custom to a person.
   It's like, oh, anybody can get this. It's available for everyone". Já
   "you've got" fala direto com a pessoa: "it feels custom like I have this
   discount. What did I do to earn this?"
2. **Loss aversion** — "by saying I have this discount, you've got it like I
   have it plays into loss aversion because if I close out of this form I lose
   that discount. So it's like I'm giving something up".

Ele data a recomendação: "I always highly recommend using wording like this or at
least right now this is what's working" (L1044).

# Botão de recusa

> **No thanks, I'll pay full price**

Versão branda: **No thanks** (L1048). Racional da versão agressiva: "now you're
really setting people up here because it's like do I want 10% off or do I want to
pay at full price? You're making them say no thanks I'll pay at full price. Some
people can think it's aggressive, but you can change it if you want, but I like
to do it in every chance that I can because it always converts better" (L1049).

No passo de SMS a recusa muda de alvo — as alternativas que ele dá são
**No thanks, just email is fine** e **No thanks, I don't want texts**, e nos dois
casos "people will still get the discount" (L1067). Ele prefere manter a versão
"pay full price" também no SMS para pressionar a adesão (L1067).

# Passo de SMS

> **Finish signing up with text to activate your discount**

Racional em duas partes (L1068-1069): "finish signing up" porque "it then shows
that they're like still in the process"; e "usually the wording of **activate**
is what works the best for getting people to follow through with SMS". Variante
que ele cogita no mesmo trecho: trocar *discount* pelo valor — "to activate your
10% off rather than saying discount just so they're reminded" (L1069).

# Pergunta do quiz

> **To claim your discount, tell us what you're shopping for**

"that's usually the wording that we see work the best. It's as little words as
possible, but it confirms that if you want this discount, you need to answer this
question" (L1057-1058).

# Página de sucesso

> **Welcome in** · **Keep shopping**

E a instrução, não o cupom: "Typically, we're going to tell people to check their
device, check their email, cuz then we can get engagement rates on those first
messages, which is going to help with our deliverability and our reputation"
(L1070-1071).

# O que ele manda deletar

Copy que ele apaga na demonstração, por nome (L1036-1038):

> ~~Save on your first order when you get email only offers~~ — "That is
> unnecessary. People know what they're doing when they sign up."
> ~~How would you like to hear from us?~~
> ~~SMS only deals~~ — "who's really doing that? And it's really not that
> common."

# O que o corpus não diz

Não há variante em português, nem versão para oferta em valor fixo ($ off) das
frases acima — todos os exemplos usam 10% off. Também não há copy declarada para
free shipping, free gift ou spin-to-win.
